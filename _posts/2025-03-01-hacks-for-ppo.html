Value Optimization Holds the Secret
Based on arXiv:2503.01491 (ByteDance Seed) • Study Note Visualization

Abstract: While Reinforcement Learning (RL) has driven recent reasoning breakthroughs (like DeepSeek-R1), standard PPO often "collapses" in long Chain-of-Thought tasks. This article explores the mathematical root causes—Value Initialization Bias and Reward Signal Decay—and visualizes why a new method, VC-PPO, succeeds by decoupling policy and value optimization.

The Mystery: Why does PPO fail?

In the era of "System 2" thinking models, the goal is to generate long chains of reasoning (CoT) spanning thousands of tokens. Recent approaches like GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) have succeeded by bypassing the value model entirely, relying on group-based statistics.

Standard PPO, however, struggles. It tends to suffer from length collapse: the model stops reasoning early, outputting short, incorrect answers.

Why care about PPO if GRPO works? GRPO provides response-level feedback. PPO offers token-level value estimation, which—if fixed—offers finer-grained credit assignment for complex logic.

GRPO uses outcomes of a group of outputs to estimate baseline. PPO uses a learned Value network $V(s)$.

Root Cause 1: The Math of Silence (Signal Decay)

The first culprit lies in how PPO calculates the Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE). In standard RL (like robotics), we use a discount factor $\lambda < 1$ to reduce variance.

The reward signal propagating from the final answer (at time $T$) to a reasoning token (at time $t$) decays exponentially:

$$ \text{Signal}_t \propto \lambda^{T-t} \cdot r_{\text{outcome}} $$

In classic tasks, $T$ is small. in Long-CoT, $T$ can be 1,000+.

Mathematical Insight

In standard RL (Mujoco/Atari), rewards accumulate constantly, so variance is high. We need $\lambda < 1$ to smooth it out. In CoT reasoning, rewards are sparse (only at the end). There is no intermediate noise to smooth out! Therefore, variance reduction on the Value function is actually harmful bias.

Root Cause 2: Value Initialization Bias

The second problem is how the Value model ($V$) is initialized. Typically, $V$ is initialized from the Reward Model ($R$).

  • Reward Model ($R$): Trained to score complete responses at the EOS token. It assigns low scores to incomplete prefixes.
  • Value Model ($V$): Needs to predict expected future return.

When $V \approx R$ at initialization, $V(s_t)$ (for an early token) is artificially low. This creates a distorted advantage:

$$ \delta_t = r_t + \gamma V(s_{t+1}) - V(s_t) $$

Because $V(s_t)$ is incorrectly low, $\delta_t$ becomes erratic (often positively biased for the wrong reasons), leading the policy to collapse to shorter sequences where the RM's scoring is more stable.

The Solution: VC-PPO

The paper proposes Value-Calibrated PPO, which introduces two specific fixes:

1. Value Pretraining (Fixing Bias)

Before RL begins, freeze the policy and train the Value model using Monte Carlo estimates ($\lambda=1$). This aligns $V$ with the true expected returns of the policy, eliminating the "RM initialization bias."

2. Decoupled-GAE (Fixing Decay)

This is the critical innovation. The paper argues that Policy and Value have different needs:

  • Value Head: Needs unbiased estimates to learn true long-term value. Use $\lambda_{\text{critic}} = 1.0$.
  • Policy Head: Still needs low variance to stabilize updates. Use $\lambda_{\text{actor}} \approx 0.95$.
$$ A^{\text{policy}} \leftarrow \text{GAE}(\lambda=0.95) $$ $$ V^{\text{target}} \leftarrow \text{Returns}(\lambda=1.0) $$

Empirical Results

Tested on Qwen2.5-32B with math reasoning tasks (AIME, GPQA).

Method Context AIME pass@1 GPQA
Standard PPO 8K 5.6% (Collapse) -
GRPO (DeepSeek-R1 logic) 16K 38.9% 49.4%
VC-PPO (Ours) 16K 48.8% 48.8%

VC-PPO outperforms GRPO by ~10% on AIME, proving that PPO's token-level optimization is superior to group-level optimization if the value estimation is stabilized.

Conclusion

The "collapse" of PPO in reasoning tasks isn't a fundamental flaw of the algorithm, but a mismatch between hyperparameters (designed for dense-reward robotics) and the sparse-reward nature of reasoning. By pretraining value and decoupling GAE, we unlock the full potential of token-level RLHF.
